## Why this data relates to Kazakh product reviews

Kaspi.kz and 2GIS are where Kazakh-speaking customers actually review goods and
services, and they moderate profanity away — which is exactly why the positive class
cannot be collected there. Unmoderated Telegram comments supply real profanity in the
same register (short, emotional, mixed KZ-RU), while Kaspi/2GIS supply review syntax
and "angry but clean" negatives. Templates then put real profane phrases inside review
frames. The test set therefore mimics moderation input rather than a marketplace dump:
92 real positives and 56 synthetic rows out of 399.

## Labels inherited from other datasets

No source labels were used as our label. Where a source had labels, they were checked
against this task and revised:

| Source | What its label actually means | What we did |
|---|---|---|
| `Rustem-Kaimolla/kazakh-swear-words` | `label` + `category` (vulgar / offensive / insult) + `severity` | kept only severe/vulgar obscenities as positives; its 9 "insult" entries (`ақымақ`, `сасық`, `қоқыс`, …) were **relabelled 0** and reused as hard negatives; 3 of its 63 sentences were dropped as mislabelled (`Щщс`, `667`, `Салдақы`) |
| `kkenbbi/kazakh_reviews_2gis` | `label` is **sentiment** (1 = positive), not profanity | ignored; every row re-labelled by our rules (all 126 turned out non-profane) |
| `datascience2201/kaspi_reviews1` | star rating 1–5 | ignored; every row re-labelled |
| `kurumikz/telegram-corpus-russian-kazakh` | no labels | weak supervision from the lexicon, then manual keep/drop of every in-scope positive |

## Personal data

* No author name, user id, date, profile link or contact detail is stored in
  `data/processed/`; `mask_pii()` replaces them with `<user>`, `<url>`, `<email>`,
  `<phone>`, `<num>` before the text is written.
* An automated audit (`python scripts/14_pii_audit.py`) scans every published text for
  e-mails, URLs, `tg://` links, @mentions, phone numbers, long digit runs and markdown
  links: **0 findings over 2,399 texts**.
* First names of staff praised inside a review body (`…рахмет, Линда!`) remain part of
  the review text: they are content, not an account, and cannot be linked to a
  reviewer. If your policy is stricter, mask them with the same `mask_pii` step before
  publishing — the labels do not depend on names.



## Split construction

1. Exact duplicates removed on a normalised key (case, homoglyphs, separators folded).
2. Near-duplicates found with char 3–5-gram TF-IDF cosine ≥ 0.85 and merged by
   union-find; **the whole cluster is assigned to one split**, so paraphrases cannot
   leak across train/validation/test.
3. 60/20/20 split, stratified on (label × synthetic) at cluster level, seed 42.

| Split | rows | positives | synthetic | real positives |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| train | 1,201 | 419 | 169 | 274 |
| validation | 400 | 141 | 57 | 92 |
| test | 399 | 140 | 56 | 92 |

Source mix per split is preserved automatically by the cluster-level assignment, e.g.
the test split has 290 Telegram rows, 56 synthetic, 27 2GIS, 24 Kaspi, 2 seeds.

## Known limitations

* **Synthetic share.** 35 % of positives are generated, so recall on generated syntax
  is optimistic; the real-only test block is reported separately in
  `reports/metrics.json → models.*.test_real_only`.
* **Domain.** Telegram chat is not a product review; Kaspi/2GIS are.
* **Language.** Pure-Russian comments are out of scope by design; do not deploy this
  on Russian-only traffic without retraining.
* **Licence.** The Telegram corpus is CC BY-NC-SA 4.0, which makes this dataset and
  the model trained on it **non-commercial**.
